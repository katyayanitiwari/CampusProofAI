# Scholarship offer safety checks

CampusProof uses these wording checks as prompts for further verification. A match is not a fraud finding, and no match does not prove an offer is genuine.

## Checks shown in verification receipts

- Requests for fees, deposits, processing charges, redemption, release, or courier payments before receiving an award.
- Notices that announce a selection or ask the recipient to claim an award, especially when the student did not apply.
- Urgent language such as “act now” or a short response deadline.
- Requests to send OTPs, passwords, PINs, card security codes, bank details, or identity details through a message or link.
- Social-media offers promising an easy award with few or no requirements.
- Guaranteed selection or guaranteed award wording.
- A message claiming to be from an Indian government scheme while linking outside `.gov.in` or `.nic.in`; treat this as a reason to cross-check, not proof of fraud.

## What students should do

1. Find the sponsor’s official website independently; do not rely only on a message link.
2. Compare the exact programme name, deadline, eligibility, contact details, and application route with the official page.
3. Do not send OTPs, passwords, PINs, or card security codes in response to an offer.
4. Verify payment requests before paying. The U.S. Department of Education OIG advises students never to pay to apply for a scholarship; India’s PM-USP Central Sector Scheme FAQ says application on the National Scholarship Portal is free. Rules may differ by programme, so check the official scheme page.
5. If evidence is missing or conflicting, contact the sponsor using independently sourced contact details.
